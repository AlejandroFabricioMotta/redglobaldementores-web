Academic Specialty Programme · Niche · English

Expert Mentor in Project Management

Tools, methodologies and good practices to plan, execute and close projects with success and lasting value.

Specialty training for those who are already «Expert Mentors». «A successful project is no coincidence; it is the result of vision, planning and leadership.»

Who it is for

Structuring what you already know

People who enter this training have already obtained their «Expert Mentor» certification. That is why what you will find here is a very specific specialty, aimed at a market niche.

You are not here to learn how to be a mentor, but to structure what you already know. Academia Global developed the «Niche Manuals» to show the Expert Mentor how to approach some of the problems specific to that niche.

Niche Manual

Your reference manual

Manual of the Expert Mentor in Project Management

This manual does not train Project Managers: it is born at the crossing of the person who leads projects, the RGM Professional Mentor and those who accompany people who already know how to decide. It covers who is who, the mentor’s position, the terrain that is read (project, organisation, person and stakeholder ecosystem), the thresholds specific to project management and the path towards discernment, autonomy and legacy.

Manual contents

What this manual covers

Part I · Who is who

  • 1. The Project Manager: the person behind the role
  • 2. The RGM Professional Mentor: a reminder of identity
  • 3. The Mentor of Project Managers: the intersection and its risk
  • 4. Who is being mentored? The decision-maker, the PMO leader and the limits of intervention

Part II · The Mentor’s position

  • 5. Position, not behaviour
  • 6. The silence of the competent
  • 7. The mentor’s anecdote: experience as a resource and as an obstacle
  • 8. Ethics, confidentiality and triangulations in project contexts

Part III · The terrain that is read

  • 9. Project variables: the conditions of deciding
  • 10. Organisation variables: the field of power
  • 11. Person variables: who decides
  • 12. The project ecosystem: stakeholders as a field of consequences

Part IV · The thresholds

  • 13. The concept of Threshold applied to project management
  • 14. Threshold I. The call: asking for commitment to something that cannot be paid for
  • 15. Threshold II. The decision-maker without an optimum: deciding when every alternative implies loss
  • 16. Threshold III. Legitimacy: leading without formal authority in someone else’s field of power
  • 17. Threshold IV. Non-delegable responsibility: when the project depends on people who are not up to it

Part V · Discernment, autonomy and legacy

  • 18. Transferring judgement without advising someone who already knows how to decide
  • 19. The progression of autonomy and the two closings
  • 20. Supervision and care of the Mentor of Project Managers
  • 21. The Mentor’s legacy in Project Management
The pathway

Four steps to your monograph

  • 1. Read the manual as a reference
    Go through it with the eyes of someone who already practises mentoring: the manual does not teach you the craft, it shows you how this niche is approached.
  • 2. Choose a problem you have lived through
    Think of a real process in which you accompanied a mentee, with your mentoring model, through a situation specific to this niche.
  • 3. Develop your monograph
    Describe the approach: the dynamics, the tools and all the resources needed for mentoring practice.
  • 4. Submit and get certified
    Send your final monograph to Academia Global by email; its approval is your certification process.
Your deliverable

The monograph

The certification candidate uses the niche manual as a reference and develops a monograph to deliver to Academia Global.

In it, they describe the approach to a problem they have lived through with their mentoring model in order to accompany a mentee through it, developing the dynamics, tools and all the resources needed for mentoring practice.
