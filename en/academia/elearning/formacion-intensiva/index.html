Training stage

Characteristics of mentoring sessions

In this video you will find the fundamental concepts every mentor must understand in order to pass on their wisdom effectively to their mentee.

Since you come from other guidance disciplines —such as psychotherapy, coaching, consulting, advising, teaching or facilitation— it is natural that you bring with you approaches and methodologies from those practices.

At Academia RGM, our aim at this stage is for you to clearly identify the specific characteristics of Mentoring as a discipline, and learn to apply your valuable empirical knowledge without falling into personal projection onto the mentee. This learning is key to practising mentoring professionally, respecting the principles that distinguish it and ensuring an authentic, enriching relationship with each mentee.

Storytelling, the most effective method to transfer wisdom in mentoring

Storytelling is one of the most effective tools a mentor uses: it consists of sharing, in the first person, a life experience that leaves a valuable lesson.

At this stage we ask you to record a video of no more than 5 minutes in which you tell a personal experience and the lesson you took from it. To ensure quality, we recommend good lighting, clear sound and recording in landscape (horizontal) format.

Case analysis

What is a “Case Analysis” for a mentoring professional?

Case Analysis is a reflective and methodological practice in which the mentor reviews, evaluates and documents the essential aspects of a mentoring session or process carried out with a client. Its purpose is to understand in depth what happened, identify patterns, review the effectiveness of the interventions and ensure that the process stays within the ethical, professional and methodological framework of the discipline.

In simple terms: it is the professional exercise that lets the mentor turn a guidance experience into accumulated knowledge, technical learning and continuous improvement.

The analysis includes elements such as:

  • Client context
  • Objectives and agreements established
  • Mentor’s observations
  • Interventions applied
  • Client’s reactions or progress
  • Evaluation of the process
  • Dilemmas, risks or critical decisions
  • Mentor’s learnings
  • Next steps

It is a key tool in the training and professional practice of the mentor.

Certification exam deliverable

Value proposition and value map

In this video you will find the fundamental concepts every mentor must understand in order to distinguish and select those life experiences that will underpin the wisdom from which the mentor will build their value propositions.

If we understand that the value proposition is the benefit the mentee will obtain from the mentor’s intervention and guidance, it will play a very important role in building the professional image. In turn, every value proposition must have its value map: nothing more and nothing less than the process, model or methodology the mentor will follow to guide the mentee towards that desired benefit.

At this stage we ask you to write your value propositions so that you can submit them as part of your certification exam.
