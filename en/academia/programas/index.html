Academic architecture in mentoring

A tiered ecosystem

Five levels of depth, workload and scope

Academia Global RGM offers a tiered training ecosystem that allows universities and training centres to include mentoring as an academic discipline.

  1. University Course Module40–60 hConceptual introduction
  2. Intensive Programme120 hCertification for experienced professionals
  3. Intermediate Programme180 hCertification for professionals WITHOUT prior experience
  4. Comprehensive Programme360 / 600 hThe essence of Mentoring and complementary knowledge
  5. Niche MentoringComprehensive + 240 hComprehensive Programme + Niche Particularities

University course module on mentoring

40–60 hours

Nature

An introductory, cross-cutting training space that brings mentoring in as a humanistic discipline within undergraduate or postgraduate degrees. It does not qualify participants to practise professionally: it develops judgement, awareness and conceptual understanding.

Learning objectives

  • Understands the historical and philosophical origin of mentoring.
  • Distinguishes mentoring from coaching, consulting, psychotherapy and other disciplines.
  • Brings an ethical and reflective outlook to their future professional practice.
  • Takes on mentoring as a cross-cutting lens in any profession.

Format and fit

In person or synchronous online. It can be included as an elective or compulsory course, a cross-cutting humanities subject or a module within professional degrees.

Outcome. It builds judgement. It does not qualify participants to practise professionally as a Mentor.

Intensive Mentoring Programme

120 hours · eLearning

Nature and audience

Specialised certification for professionals who already have experience in human accompaniment: psychologists, coaches, consultants, teachers, therapists and anyone who already knows how to lead one-to-one sessions. Purpose: to organise, reframe and professionalise their practice within the framework of mentoring.

Key features

  • 100% asynchronous online.
  • No supervised practice (previous experience is assumed).
  • Integrative final assessment.
  • Based on the Guía Esencial de la Mentoría.

Competences and fit

Graduates distinguish mentoring from other disciplines, bring the role of Mentor into their practice and position themselves professionally from their experience. It fits as a postgraduate course, a university professional certification or a continuing-education programme.

Outcome. It qualifies participants with previous track records to practise professionally as a Mentor.

Intermediate Mentoring Programme

180 academic hours

Nature and audience

A journey through the history of mentoring, the thresholds of life and their reflection, and the generation of transferable Wisdom, culminating in Applied Mentoring with 60 hours of practice supervised by the Teacher Mentor.

Purpose and structure

  • Understand mentoring as a distinct professional discipline.
  • Build ethical and methodological judgement and an initial identity as a Mentor.
  • Design and lead basic structured accompaniment processes.
  • Core bibliography: Mentoring as a Human Path.

Format and graduate profile

In person or synchronous online. Intensive format of 6 months or regular format of 9 to 12 months. Graduates practise mentoring from a solid initial level, lead basic structured processes and act with ethical judgement. It is the bridge programme of the ecosystem: stand-alone training, an entry point to the Comprehensive Programme and a basis for the niche diplomas.

Outcome. A training base sufficient for the responsible exercise of the role, equivalent to the usual university diplomas in Ibero-America.

Comprehensive Mentoring Programme

360 h · 9 months / 600 h · 18 months

Nature and audience

Complete, structural training in professional mentoring for people with no previous training in human accompaniment who wish to train from scratch with academic rigour.

Structure

  • Level 1 – Practitioner Mentor
  • Level 2 – Professional Mentor
  • Level 3 – Expert Mentor

Methodology and fit

Structured theory classes, experiential training, supervised practice, assessments at each level and the building of a professional identity. It can be framed as a higher technical diploma, professional specialisation, extended diploma or postgraduate pathway not leading to a degree.

Outcome. It trains professional mentors from scratch with a complete structure.

Niche Programmes / Expert Programmes

240 hours · 9 months (prior base of 360 h)

Nature and audience

Advanced specialisation by area of application, for already trained mentors who want to specialise in a particular field.

Areas of application

  • Human Development
  • Business and Enterprises
  • Education
  • Sport and Entertainment
  • Technology and Science
  • Public Administration
  • Society and Family
  • Culture and Arts
  • Health
  • Environment
  • Expert Workers
  • Trades and Technical Skills

Structure and fit

  • Level 1 – Practitioner Mentor
  • Level 2 – Professional Mentor
  • Level 3 – Expert Mentor
  • Level 4 – Speciality (Niche)

General mentoring training (if not previously taken), area-specific training and supervised professional practice. It is framed as a university specialisation diploma, an expert postgraduate programme or an advanced professional pathway.

Outcome. It qualifies participants as an Expert Mentor in a specific area.

Invitation

Universities and training centres

Bringing mentoring in as an academic discipline means anticipating the future.

Higher education is going through a historic moment of profound transformation. Institutions are no longer called on only to pass on technical knowledge, but to train professionals with judgement, awareness, maturity and the ability to accompany complex human processes. In this context, mentoring emerges as a strategic discipline for the twenty-first century.

Engineers, doctors, lawyers, business people, educators and public managers make decisions that affect other people. Training them in mentoring raises the human standard of professional practice.

Why bring mentoring into your institution?

  • It humanises professional practice.
  • It develops judgement and discernment.
  • It strengthens emotional and ethical maturity.
  • It improves decision-making.
  • It has a positive impact on organisations and communities.
  • It fits across all professions.

Comprehensive institutional support

Academia Global RGM does not replace the university: it works with it strategically.

  • Academic and pedagogical support.
  • Its own, carefully curated reading list.
  • Training of Teaching Mentors.
  • Help with regulatory compliance.
  • Complementary international certification.
  • Integration into a global network active on all five continents.

The institution keeps its academic autonomy while gaining access to a unified, internationally recognised conceptual framework.
